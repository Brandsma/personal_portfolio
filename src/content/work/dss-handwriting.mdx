A research project applying computer vision to one of the most studied manuscript corpora in history. The Dead Sea Scrolls present a genuinely hard low-data problem: there are a handful of scribes, a handful of surviving documents, and enormous historical and religious stakes attached to the attribution questions.

The task was twofold: handwriting recognition (transcription of damaged characters) and scribe attribution (which scribe wrote which section). The challenge is extreme data scarcity — you can't augment your way to a large dataset when the source material is two millennia old.

The approach drew on few-shot learning and domain-adapted features trained on synthetic degradation of existing manuscripts. Scribe attribution used a metric learning approach rather than a classifier, which generalises better when the number of known scribes might change.

This work was part of a broader digital humanities collaboration.
